Off Record

A snowbound, neon-soaked city block where every citizen lives a full, simulated life, and one of them is a killer. You’re a private investigator working the cases the precinct won’t touch.

The case file

Squishtech Industries
Platform
Windows 10 / 11, 64-bit
Players
1–4, online co-op
Genre
Detective, immersive sim, noir

Off Record is a first-person detective game built on one rule: nothing is scripted. Every playthrough generates a block full of people with homes, jobs, routines, relationships, grudges and secrets. When a murder happens, it happens: the killer really walks to the scene, the victim really answers the note that lured them there, the neighbours really hear the shot through the floor, and somebody really finds the body the next morning. The case you investigate is whatever the simulation left behind. Witnesses don’t read from a script. They tell you what they remember, and memory fades, blurs and gets retold. A tenant might swear she saw a tall man in a dark coat when it was a woman in navy. Gossip spreads what people heard secondhand. And some of them will lie to you: the killer, of course, but also the lovers covering an affair that has nothing to do with the murder. You don’t get a quest marker. You get a notebook, a fingerprint kit, a city directory and your own judgement. When you’re sure, make the accusation. Name the right person and back it with evidence that genuinely points to them, or they walk. Solve it, and the city doesn’t wait: tomorrow there’s another body. Leave it open for three days, and the killer, emboldened, strikes again.

  • Nothing is scripted. Every playthrough generates a block of citizens with homes, jobs, schedules, relationships, grudges and secrets, and one of them becomes a killer.
  • Witnesses who only know what they saw. Interviews draw on each person’s own fading memory and secondhand gossip, never the truth. Some people lie, and not only the killer.
  • Real forensics. Dust anything you pick up for fingerprints and match them against prints you take. Every citizen writes in their own hand: compare a sample to that unsigned note yourself.
  • No quest markers. A notebook, an evidence kit, a city map and a public directory. Accuse only when you can prove it: the right person, and two pieces of evidence that point to them.
  • The city doesn’t wait. Solve a case and tomorrow brings another body. Leave one open for three days and the killer strikes again, often at the witness who saw them.
  • A town you learn. The streets stay; which building stands where, what’s in its basements and who lives there changes every game. Every building can be entered, rooftops included.
  • People who live their day. Citizens walk their schedules through the streets and halls; lamplit windows go dark as the block goes to bed.
  • Firearms, with consequences. Seven guns, aiming down the sights, recoil and reloads. Shots are heard through the building, and shooting the wrong person costs you.
  • Co-op for up to four. Share one city, one case file and one set of evidence.
  • Winter neon noir. Falling snow, rain-slick cobbles mirroring the signs, magenta haze, steaming grates and a noir jazz score.

Was the case fair: could you solve it from what you found? Where did you get stuck? Did a witness say something that made no sense? Bugs, crashes and frame rates. Press F1 in game to send a report; the main menu’s benchmark adds your performance results to it. The case list’s Show solutions box reveals the answer after you’ve played.

Unzip anywhere and run OffRecord\OffRecord.exe, keeping the folder together. From then on the game updates itself: it checks for a newer build each time it starts and installs it on the next launch. Your saves and settings are kept. The playtest isn’t code-signed yet: if Windows says it protected your PC, choose More info → Run anyway. In co-op, every player needs the same build.
